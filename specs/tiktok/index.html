Where two numbers are given, the first is what stays visible and the second is what the field accepts. They are different questions: copy over the cap is rejected at upload, copy over the visible limit runs and gets cut off. Most guides publish only one of the two.

The 1,000 matched-user floor
  • custom audience minimum: 1000 — TikTok: "There is a requirement to have 1,000 total matched users in a Custom Audience to use it in an ad group." This is a floor on the audience itself, not on the ad group — a customer list that matches fewer than 1,000 users cannot be used at all, so a small business's list will often not qualify. Say so rather than recommending it.

These are the numbers that decide whether a campaign delivers at all, and none of them produce an error message when you cross them.

For You feed — in-feed video (non-Spark) — Body text

TikTok's own in-feed specification page states no character count for ad text — it was checked and the number is not there. So none is asserted. Write the caption short because it sits over video and truncates behind a 'See more', but do not treat any specific number as TikTok's published limit. TikTok does state the caption is "Displayed in white with a uniform font that can't be customized" and "Does not support any clickable links, symbols (@), or hashtags".

For You feed — Spark Ad (boosted organic post) — Body text

TikTok: ad captions are "Extracted directly from the organic video captions. A maximum of 4 lines can be displayed". You do not write this copy at ad level — it comes from the post. Four lines is not a character count and is not enforced here.

Why an ad group here refuses to spend

TikTok punishes anything that looks like an ad, so the usual variant grid — same footage, four headlines — tests the wrong axis. The thing that moves performance here is whether the creative reads as a native post, and that is a property of the whole video rather than of a line of copy. Test creative concepts against each other, not captions. Also: ad captions cannot contain clickable links, @ symbols or hashtags, so any copy that leans on one will be rejected or read as broken. And Spark Ads run against a real organic post, which means the comment thread is public and permanent — the same exposure Reddit has.
