Where two numbers are given, the first is what stays visible and the second is what the field accepts. They are different questions: copy over the cap is rejected at upload, copy over the visible limit runs and gets cut off. Most guides publish only one of the two.

The 300-account floor, per ad set
  • minimum audience size: 300 —

These are the numbers that decide whether a campaign delivers at all, and none of them produce an error message when you cross them.

Feed — single image — Headline

LinkedIn: "Use up to 70 characters to avoid truncation (200 character maximum)."

Feed — single image — Body text

LinkedIn calls this Introductory text: "Use up to 150 characters including spaces, emojis, and punctuation to avoid truncation (3,000 character maximum)."

Feed — single image — Description

LinkedIn: "Use 100 characters to avoid truncation (300 character maximum)." The description does not render in every placement — do not put a claim there that the ad depends on.

Feed — carousel — Headline

LinkedIn states no character count for a card headline, only that "Headline text for each image card is a maximum of two lines before being truncated". Two lines is not a number this app can enforce, so no limit is asserted — keep card headlines short and check them in preview.

Feed — carousel — Body text

LinkedIn: "Use up to 150 characters to avoid truncation on some devices (a maximum of 255 characters)". Note this 255 cap is far tighter than the single image ad's 3,000 — the same introductory text will not carry across both formats.

Right rail — text ad — Headline

LinkedIn: "Ad headline: Use up to 25 characters, including spaces." This is a hard cap, not a truncation guideline.

Small audiences and the cost of splitting them

LinkedIn audiences are small and expensive. The minimum audience is 300 member accounts and B2B targeting stacks land near that floor fast, so a four-cell test matrix that would be routine on Meta can leave every cell too thin to read. Prefer two cells over four, expect to wait weeks rather than days for a signal, and say plainly when a split would divide an audience below a size that can ever reach significance.
